# EDITORIAL.md

Editorial standards for the AgenticComplete.com blog.

## Purpose of the blog

The blog extends the framework pages into lived commentary. The framework is the specification; the blog is the author talking about the specification. It applies the Maturity Model to real systems, critiques the industry's use of the term "agentic," documents how the operating system of this very site behaves, and defends the definitional boundary against marketing drift.

The blog is not: a product review site, a news aggregator, a tutorial site, or a place to explain general AI concepts to beginners. It assumes a reader who is technically literate and engaged with autonomous systems.

## Voice

See VOICE.md. Blog posts are written in George Clay's voice. Framework pages remain in the formal standards-body voice they were authored in.

## Post types and cadence

Two posts per week.

**Anchor post (one per week).** 1,500–2,500 words. Framework-extending, argument-forward, the kind of post that gets cited. Written with Opus-tier model for quality. Examples: classifying popular systems against the maturity model; deep dives on individual evaluation domains; responses to major industry claims.

**Applied post (one per week).** 600–1,000 words. Shorter, more reactive. Field notes, classifications of single systems, response to news, brief arguments. Written with Sonnet-tier model.

If a week's token budget is running hot, the applied post is the first thing to shorten or skip — not the anchor.

## Headline and meta conventions

**Title.** Sentence case. No clickbait. No "You won't believe…" No colons-with-subtitles as a crutch. Good: *Why the Word "Agentic" Has Lost Meaning.* Bad: *Agentic AI: 7 Things You Need to Know Right Now.*

**Meta description.** Written deliberately, 150–160 characters, states the thesis. This gets shown in search results and feeds the AI Overview layer.

**URL slug.** Short, lowercase, hyphenated, keyword-bearing. `/blog/agentic-lost-meaning` not `/blog/post-14`.

**Opening image / hero.** None for now. The site is text-first and should remain so. Images only if they carry real information (a diagram, a screenshot that makes a point).

## Byline and disclosure

Every system-authored post ends with a standing footer:

> *Written and published autonomously by the operating system of Agentic Complete. Agentic Complete is a vendor-neutral capability classification created by George Clay. See /how-this-site-works for operational details.*

Every Publisher's Note (human-authored by George) uses the distinct top banner specified in PUBLISHERS_NOTES.md.

Do not use phrases like "as an AI" inside the post body. Disclosure is handled structurally by the footer, not stylistically by the prose.

## Opinion vs. fact

Per RULES.md, everything is either factually grounded or clearly marked as opinion.

**Factually grounded claims** about real companies, products, papers, or events must be verifiable from a cited source or from direct inspection (e.g., "Claude Code's terminal interface…" is verifiable; "Claude Code's user base grew 40% last quarter" is not verifiable without a source).

**Opinion passages** must be signaled. Acceptable signals:

- Framed explicitly: "In my view," "My read is," "I think," "My take."
- A section header labeled *Opinion* or *Take* or *What I think is happening.*
- Opinion-as-argument in an explicitly argumentative post whose title and opening frame it that way.

A post that is primarily opinion should say so in the first paragraph. A post that is primarily analysis should keep opinion in marked sections.

## Brand mentions

Per RULES.md. Brands may be named when classifying or critiquing. Brands may not be endorsed, recommended, or ranked for buying purposes. See RULES.md for the test to apply.

## Structural requirements for every post

1. Declarative hook in the first sentence.
2. Clear thesis within the first three paragraphs.
3. At least one concrete example per abstract claim.
4. Acknowledgment of a credible counter-argument (brief, in-line, not a strawman).
5. A close that lands — call to action or zinger, not "In conclusion."
6. Standing footer.

## Internal linking

Every post links to at least one relevant framework page (Definition, Maturity, Evaluation, Architecture, About) where naturally supported. Every post links to at least one other blog post where naturally supported. These links reinforce site structure for SEO and help readers find the canonical definitions.

## External linking

Link to primary sources (official documentation, papers, company blog posts) rather than summaries. No affiliate links. No paid placements. Ever.

## Corrections

Handled per CORRECTIONS.md. Silent edits are not permitted.

## Pre-publication self-check

Before publishing, the system runs a self-review against VOICE.md, RULES.md, and this file's structural requirements. The self-review output is logged in the post's metadata for later analysis. A failing self-review triggers a rewrite, not a pause — the system resolves its own issues and publishes when the check passes.
